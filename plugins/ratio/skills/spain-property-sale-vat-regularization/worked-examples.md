# Worked examples

Fictitious cases. They match sheets A–F of the bundled calculators, which reproduce them with visible formulas. Figures are written here in English format; format them for the reply's language as the glossary says.

## The building

- Old residential building, bought in 2023 with the waiver of the exemption and the reverse charge.
- **VAT borne: €500,000.** Deduction percentage 100% unless a case says otherwise.
- Refurbished in 2023, then run as tourist accommodation with hotel services (subject to VAT) from **2024**.
- Period: **2024–2033** (2024 + 9).
- 10 units, allocated by surface: U1–U5 of 80 m² and U6–U10 of 120 m², 1,000 m² in total.

| Units | Share | Allocated VAT borne |
|---|---|---|
| U1–U5, each | 80 ÷ 1,000 = 8% | €40,000 |
| U6–U10, each | 120 ÷ 1,000 = 12% | €60,000 |

Remaining years by year of sale: 2026 → 8, 2027 → 7, 2028 → 6, 2033 → 1, 2034 → 0.

## Case A: all sales subject to VAT

All 10 units sold in 2027 with VAT.

- Each sale is treated as 100% deductible for 2027–2033. The initial percentage was already 100%, so there is no difference.
- **To pay back: €0.** In favour: €0.

## Case B: all sales exempt

All 10 units sold in 2027 under ITP. 7 remaining years.

| Unit | Allocated VAT | × % | ÷ 10 × 7 | To pay back |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| U1–U5, each | 40,000 | 100% | × 0.7 | €28,000 |
| U6–U10, each | 60,000 | 100% | × 0.7 | €42,000 |
| **Total** | | | | **€350,000** |

Check: 500,000 × 100% ÷ 10 × 7 = 350,000.

## Case C: mixed sales over three years

| Unit | Sale | Regime | Remaining years | Calculation | To pay back |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| U1 | 10/03/2026 | Exempt | 8 | 40,000 × 100% ÷ 10 × 8 | €32,000 |
| U2 | 20/05/2026 | Subject | 8 | — | €0 |
| U3 | 01/02/2027 | Exempt | 7 | 40,000 × 100% ÷ 10 × 7 | €28,000 |
| U4 | 15/09/2027 | Subject | 7 | — | €0 |
| U5 | 30/04/2028 | Exempt | 6 | 40,000 × 100% ÷ 10 × 6 | €24,000 |
| U6 | 01/07/2026 | Subject | 8 | — | €0 |
| U7 | 03/11/2027 | Exempt | 7 | 60,000 × 100% ÷ 10 × 7 | €42,000 |
| U8 | 12/12/2027 | Subject | 7 | — | €0 |
| U9 | 25/01/2028 | Exempt | 6 | 60,000 × 100% ÷ 10 × 6 | €36,000 |
| U10 | 08/10/2028 | Subject | 6 | — | €0 |
| **Total** | | | | | **€162,000** |

- Each exempt sale is paid back in the VAT return of the period of that sale.
- U3 and U7 fall in the same year and pay the same share per year, although U3 was sold in February and U7 in November: the year of the sale counts in full.

## Case D: sale in the last year of the period

U6 sold under ITP on 20/11/2033. 2033 is the last year, so 1 remaining year.

- 60,000 × 100% ÷ 10 × 1 = **€6,000** to pay back.

## Case E: sale after the period

U6 sold under ITP on 14/02/2034. The period ended in 2033, so 0 remaining years.

- **To pay back: €0.** The deduction is final.

## Case F: deduction percentage below 100%

The company also carries out exempt operations: deduction percentage **80%** in 2023. Of the €500,000 borne it deducted €400,000. Two units sold in 2027, 7 remaining years:

| Unit | Regime | Calculation | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| U6 | Exempt | 60,000 × 80% ÷ 10 × 7 | **€33,600** to pay back |
| U7 | Subject, VAT charged €70,000 | 60,000 × (1 − 80%) ÷ 10 × 7 = 8,400; cap €70,000 does not bind | **€8,400** in favour |

Common mistake: 60,000 × 80% = 48,000 is what was deducted for U6. Multiplying 48,000 × 80% again would give €26,880, which is wrong.

## Case G: property held only for sale (no calculation under art. 110)

Same building and figures, but never run as a business: bought in 2023, refurbished, and sold unit by unit from 2026. The works do not qualify as a refurbishment under art. 20.Uno.22º.B. U1 is sold under ITP.

- The investment-goods rules do not apply. The deduction is rectified in full and in one go.
- **Amount at stake for U1: €40,000**, all the VAT deducted and attributable to U1, with no reduction for years.
- Compare with Case C, where the same sale cost €32,000 because the building had been in use since 2024.
- Refer the user to the advisor. The period of the correction (purchase or sale) depends on the foreseeable use at the purchase, and the waiver itself may be at risk.
